# UX Design Laws

> **Source:** [Adam Hayes](https://adamhayes.xyz) — [The UX Laws in My CLAUDE.md](https://adamhayes.xyz/blog/the-ux-laws-in-my-claude-md). Adapted for [Cursor FE Kits](https://github.com/ShemaiahYaba/cursor-fe-kits).

Human-readable reference for the Cursor rule at `.cursor/rules/frontend/ux-design-laws.mdc`.

Use when designing layouts, navigation, onboarding, forms, settings, dashboards, and interactive flows. Goal: minimize confusion, reduce effort, prevent mistakes, and help users complete their task quickly.

## Quick reference

| # | Law | One-line summary |
|---|-----|------------------|
| 1 | Hick's Law | Fewer choices per screen |
| 2 | Fitts's Law | Large, easy targets |
| 3 | Jakob's Law | Follow familiar patterns |
| 4 | Proximity | Group related items |
| 5 | Miller's Law | Chunk content |
| 6 | Doherty Threshold | Feedback within ~400ms |
| 7 | Von Restorff | One primary action stands out |
| 8 | Fitts's Law | Key actions near focus |
| 9 | Serial Position | Important first and last |
| 10 | Peak-End | Strong completion states |
| 11 | Zeigarnik | Show progress |
| 12 | Prägnanz | Simplest clear hierarchy |
| 13 | Hick's Law | Sensible defaults |
| 14–15 | Postel's Law | Prevent and recover from errors |
| 16 | Similarity | Consistent patterns |
| 17 | Uniform Connectedness | Visual grouping |
| 18 | Parkinson's Law | Minimize steps |
| 19 | Tesler's Law | Progressive disclosure |
| 20 | Goal-Gradient | Visible milestones |

## Implementation checklist

1. Identify the user's primary goal.
2. Design the shortest clear path to that goal.
3. Make the next action visually obvious.
4. Remove distractions.
5. Provide immediate feedback after every interaction.
6. Prevent errors before they occur.
7. Preserve user work when something goes wrong.
8. Confirm clearly when the goal is completed.

When laws conflict, prioritize clarity, accessibility, user control, and successful task completion.
